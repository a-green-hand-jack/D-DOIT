The idea

Adapt the sampling law.
Keep the backbone fixed.

01

Training-free

No parameter updates or reward backpropagation are needed during adaptation.

02

Black-box rewards

Use reward values directly, even when the evaluator is non-differentiable.

03

Kernel-level guidance

Reweight the categorical reveal transitions where masked diffusion makes its decisions.

Method

A discrete Doob transform in three moves.

Starting from a frozen masked diffusion model, D-DOIT turns a reward function into an inference-time change of measure.

01

Propose

Draw a small set of candidate next states from the frozen reverse transition.

02

Estimate value

Complete each candidate with one prediction-head pass and query the black-box reward.

03

Reweight

Sample the next state according to the estimated Doob weights, with optional late-stage best-of-K refinement.

Takeaways

The model stays intact while the sampler adapts.

D-DOIT is designed for settings where reward evaluation is available but training a task-specific model is undesirable. Its practical sampler uses a prediction-head h-estimator and finite-candidate selection.

The paper reports reward gains together with naturalness, structural, and compute diagnostics. These measurements describe task-specific trade-offs rather than a claim of universal dominance.
